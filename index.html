Hi, I'm Yu Wang.

I am a senior at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign studying Statistics, with minors in Mathematics and Computer Science.

I approach data the way a Bayesian does: a prior is a starting point, not a conclusion, and it should be questioned and revised as new evidence arrives. Training machine-learning models, I came to see them as statistics at heart: finding patterns in data and using them to predict what comes next. The difference is emphasis. Machine learning asks whether a prediction is accurate; statistics also asks why it works and how far to trust it.

In 1801, the newly discovered Ceres was tracked for only 41 days before it was lost in the Sun’s glare. The 24-year-old Gauss fit its orbit by minimizing the sum of squared errors—least squares, still the loss function behind many models today—and Ceres was found again close to where he had predicted. Today’s AI has vastly more data and computing power, yet the core question is the same: how to draw reliable, interpretable, and verifiable conclusions from data.

That question guides my work. Across the full pipeline—from cleaning and exploring data to statistical modeling, machine learning, and optimization—I aim for results that hold up: rigorous, reproducible, useful for real decisions, and honest about whether a finding is real or merely significant.
